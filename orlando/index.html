Where Water Damage Starts in Orlando Properties

Not every water problem announces itself with a flooded floor. Here is what homeowners around Orlando phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: Small drips from a worn fitting or a tiny hole in a pipe may go unnoticed behind drywall for a long time. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: During a heavy storm, runoff may collect along the foundation faster than drains can carry it off. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. Homes across Orlando can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. In Orlando we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.

Drying Out an Orlando Property: What to Expect

Touch is a poor test for whether a wall or floor has dried. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. For that reason we rely on meters, not on feel. In Orlando we use pin meters that measure moisture inside wood and drywall, non-invasive meters that scan behind surfaces, and thermal cameras that show cool, damp patches.

Not everything dries at the same rate. Carpet and drywall respond quickly, while framing lumber, subfloor, hardwood and concrete hold on much longer. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. A span of 3 to 5 days covers most jobs, and we will tell you early if yours looks like an exception.

The expensive error is pulling equipment too soon. Damp materials sealed behind new finishes may mold, buckle or delaminate months afterward. So for every Orlando job we dry to a measured target and keep the readings on file.

Found Water in Your Orlando Home? Start Here

The hour between finding water and the crew arriving is when a homeowner in Orlando can do the most good. Safety comes before saving anything. If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of it, and switch off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. Water that came up from a drain or sewer line should be left completely alone.

Next, stop the source. Use the small valve behind the toilet or under the sink, or go straight to the main. Then move what you can carry: rugs, electronics, papers, and small furniture go to a dry room. Slide plastic or aluminum foil beneath the feet of anything too heavy to move, which stops stains and swelling.

Before mopping, record the damage on your phone, since an insurance company will ask what it looked like at the start. If the water is clean and shallow, blot what you can, and let our Orlando crew handle everything else.
