Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Miami Beach

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Hold off on the sander until every board is fully dry. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • Dark gray or black stains along seams and around nail heads
  • Planks with a rounded hump down the center and lower sides
  • Fasteners working loose, heard as squeaks where the floor was soaked
  • Mildew smell coming up through the boards
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
  • Boards shrinking apart and leaving gaps following a rushed dry out
What We Do

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. From those numbers we learn the depth of the water and if the floor can realistically be saved.

2

Remove Surface Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Monitor and Advise

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Our final advice is one of three outcomes, no further work, swapping a few boards, or refinishing the floor.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Water That Keeps Moving

Once below the boards, water moves. It can reach under cabinets, wall plates and into neighboring rooms, or drip through to the ceiling of the level beneath.

Wet Subfloor

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. If it is not dried too, the floor will cup again or grow mold from underneath.

Mold Under Boards

The space between hardwood and subfloor is dark and has no airflow. Growth there is invisible from above and shows up first as a musty smell.

Stains From Nails and Staples

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. Underneath, the wood remains wet even after the face has been dried.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

The edges come up as each board expands. You might notice haze in the topcoat. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.
