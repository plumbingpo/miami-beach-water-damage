Crawl Space Water Removal in Miami Beach

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

What It Looks Like

  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

Contaminated Water

Water under a house is often not clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Termites and Other Pests

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Our Process

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

Removing the standing water is quick, generally a matter of a few hours. The wood needs more time, most often 3 to 5 days of air movers and a dehumidifier working below the floor. Thick beams, a dirt floor with no liner, or water that sat for weeks can add time. Meter readings decide when we are done, rather than a set number of days.
Going in while water is pooled is a risk we advise against. Wiring and junction boxes often run under the floor, and water touching them can carry current. There can be sewage or bacteria from the ground in it too, plus tight quarters and stale air. Shine a light in from outside the opening if you like, and leave the rest to a trained crew.
A damp patch of soil after heavy rain is common, but standing water is not normal and should not be left. Mold and wood rot both follow when as little as one or two inches sit there raising the humidity. If water is still there a day after the rain stops, or shows up in dry weather, have it checked.
Mold can begin on damp wood and paper faced insulation within 24 to 48 hours. Little air moves down there and no sun reaches it, so natural drying is very slow. Fast extraction and dehumidification do the most to hold mold back. If we find mold on the framing, it gets cleaned and an antimicrobial is applied.
Small puddles of clean water are within reach of a shop vac. Joists and subfloor stay wet, though, since a box fan only pushes moist air in circles. Crawl spaces are also tight, dark and may have live wiring or sewage in the water. Professional pumping and drying is the safer choice once the water is more than a thin puddle.
